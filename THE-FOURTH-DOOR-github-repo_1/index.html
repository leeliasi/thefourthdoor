The Years

It takes years.Here is what happens in them.

A 90-day handover, then a steady rhythm. Scroll along it.

  1. 00 Before day one

    You apply. We diagnose. Both choose.

    Weeks, not months. Questions first, answers second. Nothing is signed until the first 90 days are planned between the three of us.

  2. 01 The first 90 days

    The handover.

    Around 50 hours of us inside the business. The numbers, the operations, the team and the pipeline diagnosed. The baseline set, the priorities agreed, the operating system installed and handed to named owners in your team. Done with you, not handed to you as a list.

  3. 02 Then, the rhythm

    Board meetings.

    Regular, in the diary, both of us in the room. The numbers on the table and the decisions written down so they stick.

  4. 03 Ongoing

    Working sessions.

    Regular sessions on the business itself: the plan, the systems, the money and the team, with whichever of us the work needs.

  5. 04 Ongoing

    Coaching.

    Regular coaching from Pete and Lee. Pete on you as the chief executive: how you lead, how you delegate, how you grow into the bigger job. Lee on the leaders below you.

  6. 05 Every quarter

    Project work. Two full days.

    Two full days a quarter on the big lever for that quarter: marketing, recruitment, systems, whatever the plan says comes next. Agreed between us, scoped and signed off by us.

  7. 06 Between all of it

    A direct line to both of us.

    We do not count hours with a stopwatch. When something needs us, we are there. It is our business too.

  8. 07 Years two and three

    The milestones land.

    Management in place. You out of the day to day. Numbers that survive a buyer's accountant. Our stake vests as they land, not before.

  9. 08 When buyers come

    You decide.

    At your price, in your year. Or not at all, and you own a business that runs without you either way.

The Structure

How the partnership works.We only do well when you do.

What the market actually paysEvery multiple below applies to adjusted EBITDA, not the profit in your accounts.
What the difference isLess

They are two different numbers, and working from the wrong one is the most common reason a founder's expectation and a buyer's offer end up miles apart.

EBITDA is the profit in your accounts, before interest, tax and depreciation. Adjusted EBITDA is that profit with your own pay reset to what it would cost to employ somebody to do your job, and one-off costs stripped out. It is the number buyers multiply. For most founder-led businesses these two numbers are not the same, so it is worth knowing which one you are holding.

2x to 4x
Adjusted EBITDA

What an owner-managed business is typically priced at, and it presses towards the bottom of that range where one customer is large, turnover is flat, or the owner is the brand.

So this just ends with private equity anyway?

Yes, and that is the point. The three doors differ by terms, not destination. Sell early and you sell from below the line, at small-buyer multiples, on their terms. Walk through the fourth door and you sell above the line, years later, at institutional multiples, to buyers who came to you, with the choice of whether to sign at all. Never sell from weakness.

Why equity instead of fees?

Because incentives should point one direction: at the value of your business on the day a buyer names a number. Consultants get paid when they advise. We get paid when it works.

How long does this take?

Years, plural, and we say so up front. A business becomes institution-ready when it runs without its founder and its numbers survive a sceptical accountant. Neither happens in a quarter. Anyone promising ninety days is selling you a strategy document with a countdown timer.

What if it isn't working?

We deal with that at the start, while everyone is getting along, not at the end. There is a review point at twelve months with a clean get-out for both sides. If either of us feels it is not working, we part as friends: the equity unwinds and comes back to you, and the fee position gets trued up fairly, in a way that reflects whether we delivered what we said we would, with the detail agreed up front in the paperwork so there is nothing to argue about later. Nobody should ever feel trapped in this, you or us.

What does it cost to apply?

Nothing but honesty. You apply, we diagnose, and both sides choose. Most conversations end right there. No partnership, no hard feelings, and you will still walk away knowing more about your business than when you arrived. If it is a no, we will tell you straight and tell you why.
